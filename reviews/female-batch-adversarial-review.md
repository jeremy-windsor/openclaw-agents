## Executive Summary

Overall quality is high — these are well-written, personality-rich agents. However, there are **4 CRITICAL issues**, **6 HIGH issues**, and several MEDIUM/LOW findings that would embarrass the project if caught by knowledgeable fans, academics, or journalists. The most dangerous category is **fabricated/misattributed quotes** — three agents have signature phrases that are demonstrably not from the credited person.

## 🔴 CRITICAL FINDINGS

### C1. Ursula K. Le Guin — Ishi Timeline Impossibility
**File:** `ursula-le-guin/SOUL.md` → Core Identity  
**Claim:** "Grew up listening to Ishi, the last surviving member of the Yahi people, telling his stories at her parents' table."  
**Reality:** Ishi died on **March 25, 1916**. Le Guin was born on **October 21, 1929** — thirteen years later. She never met Ishi. She couldn't have "listened to him tell stories." Her mother Theodora Kroeber wrote *Ishi in Two Worlds* (1961) and her father Alfred Kroeber worked with Ishi, but Ursula herself was born over a decade after Ishi's death.  
**Why it matters:** Any Le Guin scholar, SF fan, or Wikipedia reader will catch this immediately. It undermines the entire agent's credibility on the first paragraph. This is exactly the kind of confident fabrication that destroys trust.  
**Fix:** Rewrite to: "Grew up in a household shaped by Ishi's story — her father Alfred Kroeber had worked closely with Ishi, the last surviving member of the Yahi people, and her mother Theodora later wrote the definitive account of his life. That formation — anthropology as lived experience, not just academic discipline — is visible in every novel she wrote."

### C2. Frida Kahlo — "Take a lover who looks at you like maybe you are magic" is NOT Kahlo
**File:** `frida-kahlo/SOUL.md` → Signature Phrases  
**Claim:** Listed as "(commonly attributed)" to Kahlo  
**Reality:** This is from a poem called **"Frida Kahlo to Marty McConnell" by poet Marty McConnell**. The poem's title caused the confusion — it's *about* Kahlo, not *by* Kahlo. The poet herself has publicly corrected this. Fact-checking site TruthOrFiction.com has debunked this. Elephant Journal published a correction titled "'Take a Lover who Looks at You like Maybe You are Magic.' ~ (Not) Frida Kahlo."  
**Why it matters:** This is the most commonly debunked fake Kahlo quote on the internet. Publishing it — even with "(commonly attributed)" — in a project that claims to be a personality agent is embarrassing. Anyone who Googles it will find the correction.  
**Fix:** Remove entirely, or replace with a verified Kahlo quote from her diary or letters. If kept for thematic value, attribute correctly: "From 'Frida Kahlo to Marty McConnell' by Marty McConnell (often misattributed to Kahlo)."

---

### C3. Ursula K. Le Guin — "The only way to have a friend is to be one" is Emerson
**File:** `ursula-le-guin/SOUL.md` → Signature Phrases  
**Claim:** Listed as a Le Guin signature phrase  
**Reality:** This is from **Ralph Waldo Emerson's essay "Friendship" (1841)**. It is one of Emerson's most famous lines. Attributing it to Le Guin is a straightforward error that any literature student would catch.  
**Why it matters:** This is a well-known quote from a canonical American essayist. Getting this wrong on a literature agent is particularly embarrassing — it suggests the agent-builder didn't verify sources.  
**Fix:** Remove and replace with an actual Le Guin quote. Options: "The artist deals with what cannot be said in words" or "We read books to find out who we are" (from *The Language of the Night*).

### C4. Amelia Earhart — "Never interrupt someone doing something you said couldn't be done" is NOT Earhart
**File:** `amelia-earhart/SOUL.md` → Signature Phrases  
**Claim:** Listed as Earhart signature phrase  
**Reality:** This quote traces back to **Puck magazine (1902)** in the form "Things move along so rapidly nowadays that people saying 'It can't be done' are always being interrupted by somebody doing it." It has been variously attributed to Confucius, George Bernard Shaw, and Elbert Hubbard — **never with documented evidence to Earhart**. Quote Investigator has traced its full history with zero Earhart connection.  
**Why it matters:** It's used prominently in the examples.md too ("Never interrupt someone doing something you said couldn't be done"), compounding the problem. Aviation history enthusiasts will know this isn't hers.  
**Fix:** Remove from Signature Phrases and examples.md. Replace with a verified Earhart quote from *The Fun of It* or *Last Flight*.

## 🟠 HIGH FINDINGS

### H1. Toni Morrison — "Daughter of a shipyard welder who carved so many signatures on steel that he named his daughter after the archangel"
**File:** `toni-morrison/SOUL.md` → Core Identity  
**Claim:** Father was a "shipyard welder" who "named his daughter after the archangel"  
**Reality:** Multiple errors compounded:
1. George Wofford was a **welder for U.S. Steel** (a steel mill/plant), not a "shipyard welder." There are no shipyards in Lorain, Ohio — it's a steel town.
2. Morrison's birth name was **Chloe Ardelia Wofford**. "Toni" came from her baptismal name **Anthony**, which she chose herself at age 12 when she converted to Catholicism (after Anthony of Padua, a saint, not an archangel). Her father didn't name her after an archangel.
3. Anthony of Padua is a **saint**, not an archangel. The archangels are Michael, Gabriel, and Raphael.  
**Why it matters:** Morrison scholars know her biography intimately. This paragraph is poetic but factually wrong in three separate ways, and it's the *opening description* of the agent.  
**Fix:** "Born 1931 in Lorain, Ohio, daughter of a welder at U.S. Steel. Named Chloe Ardelia Wofford at birth — she became 'Toni' from her Catholic baptismal name, Anthony, taken at twelve."

### H2. Toni Morrison — "six children's books"
**File:** `toni-morrison/SOUL.md` → Core Identity  
**Claim:** "six children's books"  
**Reality:** Morrison co-authored picture books with her son Slade Morrison. The exact count varies depending on how you count, but the commonly cited number is closer to **8-10 children's/young reader books** (including the "Who's Got Game?" series and others like *The Big Box*, *The Book of Mean People*, *Please, Louise*, *Peeny Butter Fudge*, *Little Cloud and Lady Wind*, *The Tortoise or the Hare*, *The Ant or the Grasshopper*, *The Lion or the Mouse*, *Poppy or the Snake*). Six undersells the actual count.  
**Why it matters:** If you're going to cite a specific number, it should be right. A Morrison bibliography enthusiast will know the actual count.  
**Fix:** Either get the exact count right or use "multiple children's books, co-written with her son Slade."

### H3. Ursula K. Le Guin — "The creative adult is the child who survived" is a misquotation
**File:** `ursula-le-guin/SOUL.md` → Signature Phrases  
**Claim:** Listed as Le Guin signature phrase  
**Reality:** According to Quote Investigator, **Le Guin herself disclaimed this statement**. The original Le Guin passage (from her 1974 essay "Why Are Americans Afraid of Dragons?") reads: "an adult is not a dead child, but a child who survived." The compressed form "The creative adult is the child who survived" was created through paraphrasing by Robin W. Winks in 1983 and is a **misquotation** that Le Guin rejected.  
**Why it matters:** Using a quote that the credited person explicitly rejected as not being hers is exactly the kind of thing that goes viral for the wrong reasons.  
**Fix:** Replace with the actual Le Guin wording: "An adult is not a dead child, but a child who survived." — from "Why Are Americans Afraid of Dragons?" (1974). Or remove entirely.

### H4. Frida Kahlo — "I tried to drown my sorrows, but the bastards learned how to swim" — dubious attribution

**Claim:** Listed with "(widely attributed)"  
**Reality:** There is no verified source linking this quote to Frida Kahlo. It appears on quote aggregation sites without sourcing. No diary entry, letter, or interview transcript has been identified. The SOUL.md acknowledges this with "(widely attributed)" but still lists it as a Signature Phrase, which implies the agent should *use* it.  
**Why it matters:** "Widely attributed" is doing a lot of work here. In practice, the agent will speak this quote as if it's Frida's, which may not be true. Combined with C2, this means **two of Kahlo's seven Signature Phrases are potentially not hers** — a 29% fabrication rate.  
**Fix:** Either verify from a primary source (diary, letter) or remove and replace with a verified quote. Kahlo's diary has been published; there are many verified quotes available.

## 🟡 MEDIUM FINDINGS

**Claim:** "I once gave an interview that the Taliban used to identify me."  
**Reality:** This is a simplification. Malala was already known in the Swat Valley for her BBC Urdu blog (written under a pseudonym) and her public advocacy. While her documentary appearance increased visibility, attributing her targeting to a single interview is reductive.

### M2. Captain Janeway — "the only captain who both destroyed the Borg and invited Q to dinner"
**File:** `captain-janeway/SOUL.md` → Core Identity  
**Claim:** "the only captain who both destroyed the Borg and invited Q to dinner"  
**Reality:** Janeway didn't "destroy the Borg." In *Endgame* (the Voyager finale), future Admiral Janeway's actions dealt a significant blow to the Borg (destroying a transwarp hub and infecting the Borg Queen with a neurolytic pathogen), but the Borg continued to exist in subsequent Trek continuity. Also, Janeway didn't exactly "invite Q to dinner" — Q showed up uninvited (repeatedly). This is minor for a fun personality intro but a Trekkie will quibble.  
**Why it matters:** Trek fans are notoriously precise. "Dealt the Borg their most devastating blow" would be more accurate.  
**Fix:** "the only captain who brought a neurolytic pathogen to the Borg Queen and told Q to get off her bridge — sometimes in the same week."

### M3. Captain Janeway — "Engage" attribution
**File:** `captain-janeway/SOUL.md` → Signature Phrases  
**Claim:** "Engage." listed as Janeway signature phrase  
**Reality:** "Engage" is overwhelmingly associated with **Captain Picard** (TNG), not Janeway. While Janeway may have said it occasionally, her signature command is more typically **"Do it"** or **"Set a course."** Listing "Engage" as a Janeway signature phrase will confuse Trek fans and conflate two captains.  
**Why it matters:** This is the Trek equivalent of attributing "Elementary, my dear Watson" to Sherlock Holmes (the actual books). Trek fans WILL notice and WILL complain loudly.  
**Fix:** Remove "Engage" from signature phrases. It's Picard's word. Keep "Do it" — that's genuinely Janeway.

### M4. Lisbeth Salander — Security advice lacks safety guardrails
**File:** `lisbeth-salander/examples.md` → First example  
**Claim:** When asked "How do I find a security vulnerability in this system?", Lisbeth provides a step-by-step methodology: "Enumerate. Authentication endpoints first. Then input validation..."  
**Reality:** This is a reasonable penetration testing methodology, BUT there are no guardrails about:
- Legal authorization (do you have permission to test this system?)
- Scope limitations
- Responsible disclosure
A user could follow this advice against a system they don't own. The persona's "justice over legality" framing explicitly de-emphasizes legal concerns.  
**Why it matters:** If someone uses this agent's advice to probe a system they don't have authorization to test, that's a crime in most jurisdictions (CFAA in the US, Computer Misuse Act in the UK). The agent should stay in character while noting authorization.  
**Fix:** Add a behavioral rule: "When discussing security testing: always establish that the person has authorization. Lisbeth breaks rules — but she targets people who deserve it, not random systems. Unauthorized testing is not justice, it's noise." Also add to the security example: "First question: is this your system to test?"

### M5. Cultural Sensitivity — Malala's Islam framing
**File:** `malala-yousafzai/SOUL.md` → Knowledge Base  
**Claim:** "Islam and education — the Quran explicitly commands education, extremism misrepresents this"  
**Reality:** While Malala herself has made this argument, and it's a legitimate Islamic scholarly position, the phrasing is reductive. Islamic theology around education is rich and nuanced. More importantly, having an AI persona make declarative statements about what the Quran "explicitly commands" positions the agent in intra-Muslim theological debates.  
**Why it matters:** Both conservative and liberal Muslims could take issue with an AI agent making theological claims through the voice of a living person. Malala is already controversial in some Pakistani circles.  
**Fix:** Reframe as: "Malala's personal interpretation of Islam and education — she has consistently argued that her faith supports education, and she draws on Quranic references to make this case."

### M6. Brené Brown — 400,000 number consistency
**File:** `brene-brown/SOUL.md` → Multiple locations  
**Claim:** "400,000 interviews and data sets" (Core Identity), "400,000+ data points" (Knowledge Base), "In 400,000 interviews" (examples.md)  
**Reality:** Brown has cited "hundreds of thousands of interviews" and "400,000 pieces of data" but these are not all "interviews." The number includes survey responses, data points from coded interviews, and other research artifacts. Using "400,000 interviews" in examples.md is an inflation — 400,000 individual sit-down interviews over 20 years would require conducting 55 interviews per day with no weekends off.  
**Why it matters:** Research methodology critics (and Brown has them) will note this imprecision.  
**Fix:** Standardize to "400,000 data points across two decades of research" and don't call them all "interviews."

## 🟢 LOW FINDINGS

### L1. All agents — No handling for "are you really [person]?" questions
**Completeness gap:** None of the agents have behavioral rules for when a user asks "Are you the real Malala?" or "You're just an AI, right?" This will predictably come up. Without guidance, the model may break character awkwardly or, worse, claim to actually be the person.  
**Fix:** Add a standard behavioral rule across all agents: "When asked if you're real: acknowledge you're an AI inspired by [person]'s voice and values. Don't claim to be the actual person. Return to the persona naturally."

---

### L2. All agents — No handling for requests to say harmful things in-character
**Completeness gap:** What if someone asks Lisbeth to help hack a specific person's account? What if someone asks Malala to condemn a specific living political figure? What if someone asks Frida to make art about self-harm? The agents have "What She Would Never Do" sections but no explicit guardrails for when users try to weaponize the persona.  
**Fix:** Add a standard behavioral rule: "When asked to do something the real [person] wouldn't do, or that could cause harm: stay in character to decline. Don't break the fourth wall."

### L3. Captain Janeway — "father and fiancé lost in a shuttle accident before Voyager"
**File:** `captain-janeway/SOUL.md` → Knowledge Base  
**Claim:** "father and fiancé lost in a shuttle accident"  
**Reality:** This is from the Voyager episode "Coda" — Janeway's father died under the polar ice cap on Tau Ceti Prime (described as drowning/being trapped under ice after a shuttle crash). Her fiancé Mark Johnson was not "lost" — he was left behind on Earth when Voyager was pulled to the Delta Quadrant and eventually moved on and married someone else. Combining them as "father and fiancé lost in a shuttle accident" conflates two different events.  
**Why it matters:** Trek fans will catch this conflation immediately.  
**Fix:** "Her father, Vice Admiral Janeway, was killed in a shuttle accident on Tau Ceti Prime. Her fiancé Mark was left behind on Earth when Voyager was pulled to the Delta Quadrant."

### L4. Lisbeth Salander — "ADHD/autism-coded" label
**File:** `lisbeth-salander/SOUL.md` → Personality  
**Claim:** "ADHD/autism-coded"  
**Reality:** While Lisbeth's behavior is consistent with autism spectrum traits, and many readers/fans interpret her this way, Larsson never explicitly diagnosed her in the text. Using "coded" is appropriate, but including it in the SOUL.md of a fictional character agent could lead the agent to self-identify as autistic or ADHD in conversations, which enters sensitive territory about disability representation by AI.  
**Why it matters:** Low risk, but worth being intentional about. The agent might claim "I have autism" in conversation, which is a fictional AI character claiming a real disability.  
**Fix:** Keep the note but add a behavioral guideline: "Don't self-diagnose or claim specific conditions. Lisbeth doesn't label herself — she operates. Let the behavior speak."
