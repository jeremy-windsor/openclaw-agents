## 6. Ursula K. Le Guin 🌌

### Grade: B

**Factual Accuracy:**
- Born 1929 in Berkeley ✓
- Daughter of Alfred and Theodora Kroeber ✓
- Died January 22, 2018 ✓
- *The Left Hand of Darkness* (1969) ✓
- *The Dispossessed* ✓
- *A Wizard of Earthsea* ✓
- National Book Awards speech 2014 ✓ (actually it was the National Book Foundation Medal for Distinguished Contribution to American Letters)
- "We live in capitalism..." quote ✓
- Translated Tao Te Ching ✓

**Issues:**
1. 🔴 *Factual Error:* "Grew up listening to Ishi, the last surviving member of the Yahi people, telling his stories at her parents' table." **Ishi died on March 25, 1916. Le Guin was born on October 21, 1929.** She could not possibly have listened to Ishi tell stories. Alfred Kroeber did study Ishi, and Theodora Kroeber later wrote *Ishi in Two Worlds* (1961), so Le Guin grew up hearing stories ABOUT Ishi, not FROM Ishi. This is a significant factual error that needs to be corrected.
2. 🔴 *Misattributed Quote:* "The only way to have a friend is to be one." This is from **Ralph Waldo Emerson**, not Ursula K. Le Guin. It appears in Emerson's essay "Friendship" (1841). This should be removed from Le Guin's signature phrases immediately.
3. ⚠️ *Dubious Attribution:* "The creative adult is the child who survived." This is widely attributed to Le Guin on the internet but I cannot find a verified primary source in her published works. It may be misattributed. Should be marked "(commonly attributed)" or replaced with a verified quote.
4. ⚠️ *Minor:* "21 novels" — scholarly counts vary (21-23 depending on inclusion criteria). Acceptable but could be "more than 20."
5. ⚠️ *Minor:* "4 essay collections" — she published at least 5 (*The Language of the Night*, *Dancing at the Edge of the World*, *The Wave in the Mind*, *Words Are My Matter*, *No Time to Spare*). The number is low.
6. ⚠️ *Minor:* "11 volumes of short stories" — approximately correct depending on how you count collections vs. omnibuses.

**Character Consistency:** Good. The examples capture her patient, systems-thinking approach well. The *Dispossessed* metaphor for team arguments is well-chosen.

**Tone Quality:** The examples are solid but could be more playful. Le Guin's writing has genuine humor and warmth that these examples capture intellectually but could embody more viscerally. The voice is slightly too professorial — Le Guin was sharp and could be funny.

**Missing Elements:** None structurally. But the Emerson quote and Ishi error are significant enough to dock the grade.
